About OCF

The Open Computing Foundation exists to promote computing independence, software interoperability, platform accessibility and open technical research. Its concern is the continued ability to use digital technologies without unnecessary dependence on their original execution environments.

Purpose and scope

Software frequently embodies years of practical knowledge, creative work and specialised workflows. Its usefulness need not end with the commercial life of a device or runtime. OCF’s purpose is to investigate how that usefulness can be carried into environments that are broadly accessible and based on public standards.

The organisation’s principal undertaking is The Web Independence Initiative, with the central technical project codenamed Ubiquity. OCF provides the institutional purpose; the initiative defines the field of work; Ubiquity identifies the proposed technical research and development project. These names do not describe separate companies or finished products.

Founding and contributor direction

OCF was founded by Colm Lenehan. Recognition of that founding does not imply that the organisation is a personal brand or that all future decisions belong to its founder. The intended model is contributor-led: people doing the work should help determine priorities, evaluate proposals and shape the organisation’s future.

A formal governance structure, board, membership process or established contributor body is not claimed. Procedures for decision-making, stewardship and resolving disagreements would need to be developed transparently as participation takes shape.

Intended nonprofit operating model

OCF is conceived as an independent, nonprofit computing organisation, rather than a commercial software company, subscription service or application marketplace. Its intended purpose is to advance shared technical understanding and useful compatibility work.

This statement does not establish legal registration, charitable status, tax exemption, funding or partnerships. No such status or arrangements are represented on this site.

Commitment to open computing

Open computing means making interfaces, assumptions and practical constraints understandable, and allowing users meaningful choices about how they access software and retain their data. Public specifications and reproducible documentation can make implementations easier to inspect, replace and maintain.

Respect for authors’ licences, security boundaries and privacy is part of that commitment. Technical access is not permission to redistribute a program, copy its assets or circumvent its protections. See the guiding principles for the commitments intended to govern this work.
