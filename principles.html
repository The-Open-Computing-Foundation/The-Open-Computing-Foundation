Principles

These principles describe how OCF intends to conduct its work. They apply to the choices made within The Web Independence Initiative and Ubiquity as well as to the organisation’s development.

Open computing

Prefer documented interfaces, inspectable reasoning and formats that users can understand and exchange. Identify closed dependencies explicitly so that access is not confused with control.

Platform independence

Separate essential application behaviour from avoidable operating-system assumptions. Evaluate several environments and document where a particular device or platform remains necessary.

Interoperability

Preserve meaningful data exchange across implementations. Document format versions, conversion losses and protocol boundaries rather than declaring compatibility from superficial similarity.

Technical transparency

Distinguish proposals, experiments and demonstrated capabilities. Publish methods and limitations alongside successful outcomes, and correct claims when evidence changes.

Accessibility

Treat keyboard access, assistive technologies, readable interfaces and varied device capabilities as part of functional compatibility. Expanding platform access should also expand the range of people who can use the software.

Collaboration

Make technical proposals understandable enough for others to review and improve. Credit work appropriately, welcome well-supported disagreement and seek upstream cooperation where feasible.

Respect for authors and licences

Observe the separate conditions applying to source code, binaries, assets, firmware, trademarks and services. Do not treat possession of a copy as unrestricted permission to adapt or redistribute it.

Security and privacy

Respect browser security boundaries and minimise permissions and data transfers. Document trust assumptions, handle untrusted input carefully and plan for updates to vulnerable dependencies.

Long-term maintainability

Prefer bounded, documented components with tests and clear ownership of maintenance work. Record upstream versions and deployment assumptions so future contributors can reproduce and revise an implementation.

Contributor-led development

Allow people contributing research, code, documentation and testing to shape priorities and decisions. Develop governance transparently as participation grows; do not imply an established formal structure before one exists.
