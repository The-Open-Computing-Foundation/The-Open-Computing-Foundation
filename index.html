The Open Computing Foundation

The Open Computing Foundation (OCF) is an independent computing organisation whose principal undertaking is The Web Independence Initiative. Its central technical research and development project, codenamed Ubiquity, concerns the legitimate adaptation of existing software and computing environments to the open web.

Founded by Colm Lenehan, OCF is guided by a contributor-led philosophy and an intended nonprofit operating model. Its direction should be shaped by the people contributing research, software, testing and documentation. Nonprofit describes this intended model; it is not a claim of registered charitable or tax-exempt status.

The Web Independence Initiative

The initiative’s long-term ambition is to make practically everything in computing accessible, compatible with, and wherever feasible native to the web. It asks how applications, games, tools and specialised environments can become less dependent on a particular operating system, device configuration or proprietary runtime.

This is a research direction, not a claim of universal compatibility. Some software can be adapted using existing web technologies; other software would require substantial engineering, permission from its authors, or capabilities that browsers do not provide. See the initiative overview for the scope and distinctions.

Ubiquity: technical direction

Ubiquity is the initiative’s central project codename. Proposed approaches include source-level ports, WebAssembly execution, compatibility layers, lawful emulation and web-native reimplementations. No completed compatibility platform, catalogue of supported applications or released implementation is asserted here.

  • Ubiquity — software categories, architectural approaches and unresolved questions.
  • Research — browser capabilities, constraints and authoritative technical references.
  • Proposed objectives — a progression from scope definition to reproducible experiments.

Why web independence matters

Access to a useful program can disappear when its supported operating system is discontinued, its distribution channel closes, or its hardware becomes unavailable. Documents may remain readable only through one application. A standards-based execution route could help preserve access and reduce the need to maintain an entire original environment.

Moving an interface into a browser does not by itself remove dependence. A remotely streamed application may still require one provider’s servers, accounts and continuing service. The initiative therefore considers where computation happens, who controls data, and whether implementations and formats can be replaced.

Long-term direction

OCF intends to support open technical work whose assumptions and limitations can be inspected. The immediate emphasis is on defining tractable questions and documenting evidence. Broader compatibility would follow from demonstrated methods, rather than promises about every program.
